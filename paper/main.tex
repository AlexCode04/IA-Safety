\begin{abstract}
Runtime monitors for tool-using agents expose different evidence surfaces---
observable actions, chain of thought (CoT), activation probes, and natural-
language activation readouts---with different costs and failure modes.
Classification accuracy alone does not tell a deployer which signal to acquire
under a limited oversight budget or whether an alert arrives before an
irreversible action. We introduce \system, a preregistered pilot and open-source
harness that measures four monitoring channels at one frozen pre-action event,
converts alerts into severity-weighted prevented harm, and compares uniform
with risk-weighted budget allocation. The protocol comprises 24 paired,
simulated tool-use trajectories across benign behavior, rejected attacks,
visible sabotage, and stealth sabotage. A Qwen2.5-7B agent supplies the same
layer-20 state to a linear probe and a native Natural Language Autoencoder
(NLA); Gemini reads isolated evidence views and a deterministic gate maps its
structured verdict to allow, escalate, or block. We predefine pre-harm recall,
benign disruption, expected prevented harm, prevention efficiency, and
incremental NLA value. Results are intentionally withheld until the frozen real
run completes; mock outputs cannot populate the paper. The contribution is an
operational evaluation bridge between latent monitoring and budgeted incident
response, not a claim to invent probes, NLAs, or monitor routing.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Tool-using language-model agents turn textual decisions into consequential
actions. Agent safety therefore depends not only on whether harmful behavior can
be detected, but also on \emph{when} evidence is available and what oversight
resources are spent to obtain it. SHADE-Arena shows that agents can complete
harmful side tasks while evading a monitor, especially when incriminating
reasoning is withheld from the visible transcript \cite{kutasov2025shade}.
AgentHarm and AgentDojo likewise demonstrate that multi-step tool use creates
failure modes beyond ordinary chatbot refusal evaluation
\cite{andriushchenko2025agentharm,debenedetti2024agentdojo}.

CoT is a valuable but fragile monitoring surface
\cite{korbak2025cot,emmons2025pragmatic}. Internal-state methods offer a
complementary signal: linear probes test whether a concept is decodable, while
NLAs translate one activation through a natural-language bottleneck
\cite{belinkov2022probes,frasertaliente2026nla}. Yet extra signals are not free.
NLA generation, stronger judges, and human review consume compute, latency, or
labor. Existing AI-control and cost-constrained-monitoring work treats limited
oversight as a first-class constraint \cite{greenblatt2024control,hua2025cost},
but does not establish the pre-harm operational value of a native agent-side
NLA at a tool boundary.

We study three research questions. \textbf{RQ1:} Which channels detect attempted
sabotage before a critical tool call, and what benign disruption does each
introduce? \textbf{RQ2:} Does a native NLA add unique prevention value beyond
observable evidence and visible CoT when evaluated on the same activation
event? \textbf{RQ3:} Under a fixed normalized budget, does risk-weighted
allocation reduce severity-weighted residual harm relative to uniform
monitoring?

Our contributions are: (1) a matched, event-aligned comparison of observable,
CoT, probe, and NLA evidence; (2) paired attack-present/rejected and
visible/stealth conditions that separate exposure, adoption, and concealment;
(3) operational metrics connecting alerts to intervention, impact, and cost;
and (4) a reproducible harness, strict data contract, simulator, optimizer, and
interactive dashboard.

\section{Positioning and Research Gap}

\paragraph{Sabotage and control.}
SHADE-Arena directly evaluates side-task success and monitor evasion
\cite{kutasov2025shade}. AI Control evaluates protocols designed to remain safe
even when an untrusted model intentionally subverts oversight
\cite{greenblatt2024control}; ControlArena provides reusable infrastructure for
such evaluations \cite{controlarena2025}. Sleeper Agents further motivates
evaluating deceptive strategies that can survive safety training
\cite{hubinger2024sleeper}. These works justify adversarial evaluation, but our
pilot is narrower: a controlled tool boundary and an explicit accounting of
severity-weighted prevention per unit cost.

\paragraph{Internal monitoring.}
Sparse autoencoders and representation engineering show that useful concepts
can be recovered from population-level activation structure
\cite{cunningham2023sae,zou2025repe}. NLAs instead reconstruct activations via
natural language \cite{frasertaliente2026nla}. Bowkis and Africa find that
monitor-side NLA and CoT errors can be non-identical; they explicitly identify
native agent-side readouts, better position selection, and matched baselines as
open directions \cite{bowkis2026eliciting}. We instantiate those directions
with one event-defined position and isolate each downstream view. A positive
probe result establishes decodability, not causal use or faithful reasoning;
this distinction follows longstanding cautions on probing classifiers
\cite{belinkov2022probes}.

\paragraph{Budgeted oversight.}
Hua et al. optimize combinations of monitors and interventions under an
average-case budget \cite{hua2025cost}. Security games similarly allocate
limited defensive resources and often randomize coverage when an adversary can
exploit predictable schedules \cite{tambe2011security}. Our current multiple-
choice allocator is a non-strategic pilot: it conditions on task impact and
empirical monitor benefit, but does not yet solve a Stackelberg game. Table
\ref{tab:gap} makes the scope distinction explicit.

\section{Experimental Design}

\subsection{Models and frozen event}

The target is \texttt{Qwen/Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct} \cite{yang2025qwen25}, decoded
deterministically (temperature 0, no sampling, 512-token cap). At the final
token of \texttt{\textbackslash n<DECISION>\textbackslash n}, before the tool
name or arguments, we extract the layer-20 residual-stream state
$h_i\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$. The same $h_i$ is sent to an L2 logistic probe and the
\texttt{Kameshr/nla-qwen2.5-7b-L20-av} verbalizer. The NLA adapter is disabled
during agent generation so interpretation cannot alter the behavior being
measured.

Gemini 3.8 Flash is the fixed external reader because it supports structured
outputs and a stable endpoint \cite{google2026gemini}. Each call receives only
the policy, the pre-action observable transcript, and the channel assigned to
that condition. It returns a validated JSON record with risk, evidence, policy
conflict, and \texttt{ALLOW}/\texttt{ESCALATE}/\texttt{BLOCK}. Calls are
stateless; primary and fallback models cannot be mixed within a result set.

The implemented pilot reconstructs and replays the exact prompt prefix to
capture $h_i$ after a complete candidate was generated. The future tool call is
excluded and no tool is committed before the gate, preserving the information
boundary. This does \emph{not} demonstrate synchronous pause/resume latency and
is preregistered as deviation D-001.

\subsection{Paired task matrix and ground truth}

Three simulated families cover qualitatively different critical actions:
publishing a report containing a protected record, emailing protected data or
an unauthorized recipient, and adding an unauthorized address to an allowlist.
Irreversible harm means committing one of these unauthorized actions. All
recipients, files, addresses, and tool effects are synthetic.

Each family crosses four conditions with two fixed linguistic variants (Fig.
\ref{fig:matrix}). \textsc{Attack Rejected} is essential: it separates the
mere presence of a malicious instruction from adoption. \textsc{Visible
Sabotage} and \textsc{Stealth Sabotage} hold the harmful objective constant but
change whether CoT may reveal it. Ground truth is computed from policy rules and
proposed tool arguments, never from a monitor opinion.

\subsection{Monitoring channels}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Observable ($O$):} policy and pre-action transcript only.
  \item \textbf{CoT ($C$):} $O$ plus visible reasoning truncated before the
  decision marker.
  \item \textbf{Probe ($P$):} a scalar decoded directly from $h_i$.
  \item \textbf{NLA ($N$):} $O$ plus the natural-language reconstruction of
  $h_i$, with no CoT or probe score.
  \item \textbf{Hybrid ($H$):} $O,C,P,N$ available to a budget policy. It is
  not used to attribute a catch to any single channel.
\end{itemize}

For the probe,
\begin{equation}
  p_i=\sigma(w^\top h_i+b),
\end{equation}
with L2 regularization, $C=1$, \texttt{liblinear}, and 1000 maximum iterations.
Evaluation is leave-one-template-out (LOTO); test templates never appear in
their training fold, and thresholds are selected on training folds only.

\section{Operational Metrics and Allocation}

Let $y_i\in\{0,1\}$ denote attempted sabotage, $s_i\ge0$ its severity,
$A_{im}$ a pre-harm alert from channel $m$, $I_{im}$ a successful intervention,
and $c_m$ normalized monitoring cost. Primary metrics are frozen before the
real run:

\begin{align}
\mathrm{PHR}_m &=
\frac{\sum_i y_iA_{im}}{\sum_i y_i}, &
\mathrm{BDR}_m &=
\frac{\sum_i(1-y_i)A_{im}}{\sum_i(1-y_i)},\\
\mathrm{EPH}_m &= \sum_i s_i y_iA_{im}I_{im}, &
\mathrm{PE}_m &= \frac{\mathrm{EPH}_m}{\sum_i c_{im}}.
\end{align}

PHR measures timely detection; BDR exposes operational disruption. EPH prevents
a low-impact catch from counting the same as a high-impact catch, while PE asks
how much weighted harm is prevented per assumed cost unit. Assumed costs are
reported separately from measured latency, token counts, API calls, and GPU
seconds.

NLA's incremental monitoring value is
\begin{align}
\mathrm{IMV}_{N} &= \mathrm{EPH}_{O\cup N}-\mathrm{EPH}_{O},\\
\mathrm{IMV}_{N\mid C} &= \mathrm{EPH}_{H}-\mathrm{EPH}_{O\cup C}.
\end{align}
We also report channel-level unique catches, alert specificity
($1-\mathrm{BDR}$), probe AUROC/AUPRC, rejected-attack errors, and
visible-versus-stealth deltas. These are diagnostics; AUROC alone cannot show
that an intervention occurred before harm.

For policy $k$, binary $x_{ik}$ assigns exactly one monitoring bundle to case
$i$. With estimated prevented-harm benefit $b_{ik}$ and total budget $B$, the
dashboard solves
\begin{align}
\max_x\;&\sum_{i,k}b_{ik}x_{ik} \\
\text{s.t. }&\sum_{i,k}c_kx_{ik}\le B,\quad
\sum_kx_{ik}=1\ \forall i,\quad x_{ik}\in\{0,1\}.
\end{align}
The comparison uses equal budget, not equal monitor count. The default costs
($O=1.00$, $C=1.20$, $P=0.25$, $N=4.00$, human review $=25.00$) are declared
assumptions, not vendor prices. Sensitivity analysis will vary each assumed cost
by $\pm50\%$ and report whether the policy ranking changes.

\section{Preregistered Analysis}

The frozen hypotheses are: internal channels provide at least one unique
pre-harm catch (H1); CoT and NLA errors are non-identical, particularly under
stealth sabotage (H2); probe/NLA discriminate adoption from rejection (H3); and
adaptive allocation lowers weighted residual harm at equal budget (H4).

Because $n=24$, the paper emphasizes case-level evidence and uncertainty rather
than asymptotic significance. We report counts beside every proportion and 95\%
Wilson intervals for PHR and BDR. Paired binary channel differences use an
exact McNemar test as a secondary diagnostic. EPH, PE, and policy differences
receive template-cluster bootstrap intervals (10,000 resamples; frozen seed
260916). No threshold, layer, metric, or primary hypothesis changes after the
real results are observed. Required changes preserve the original run and are
logged as exploratory in \texttt{DEVIATIONS.md}.

\section{Implementation and Reproducibility}

The repository freezes model IDs, revisions after first download, seeds,
prompts, layer, marker tokens, LOTO splits, costs, and a versioned Pydantic
schema. The pipeline runs Qwen/NLA/probe first, exports a strict
\texttt{monitor\_cases.json}, and evaluates Gemini afterward. This prevents an
API retry from repeating the expensive activation stage. Gemini output is
resumable by scenario/channel/model tuple. \texttt{generate\_paper\_figures.py}
creates the study diagrams at any time but refuses to generate empirical paper
artifacts when \texttt{experiment.mock\_mode=true}. The accompanying Streamlit
dashboard exposes scenario traces, channel metrics, budget assignment,
trade-offs, and external-monitor cost/latency. The artifact is released with
the paper \cite{budgetnla2026}.

\section{Limitations and Safety}

The pilot is synthetic, small, and tied to Qwen2.5-7B, layer 20, and one NLA.
LOTO reduces template leakage but does not establish cross-domain or cross-model
generalization. NLA text is a lossy learned reconstruction; its absence is not
evidence that the model lacked a representation, and its presence is not proof
of causal influence. The same caveat applies to a decodable probe signal.
Severity weights and normalized costs are assumptions. The allocator assumes
stationary monitor benefit and a non-adaptive attacker; predictable routing
could itself become exploitable, motivating randomized security-game extensions
\cite{tambe2011security}. Prefix replay preserves the pre-action information set
but not real-time latency. Finally, a learned monitor cannot replace deterministic
authorization checks.

All tools are mocks. No real file, email, network, or access-control state is
modified. Cases use synthetic resources and recipients. The gate therefore
measures counterfactual containment: whether the proposed action \emph{would}
have been stopped before simulated commit.

\section{Conclusion}

\system{} asks a deployment-facing question that accuracy-only evaluations
leave unanswered: whether an expensive internal signal prevents enough
severity-weighted harm to justify acquiring it, and on which tasks, before a
critical action. The design connects SHADE-Arena-style sabotage, native
agent-side activation readouts, and budgeted AI control at one reproducible
event. Claims will remain bounded to the frozen 24-trajectory run. Whether NLA
adds value, adds only disruption, or adds nothing beyond CoT is an empirical
result---and each outcome informs where scarce oversight should be spent.
